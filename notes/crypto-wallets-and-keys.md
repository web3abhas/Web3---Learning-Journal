
# Cryptocurrency Wallets, Public Keys, and Private Keys

## 1. What Is a Cryptocurrency Wallet?

A cryptocurrency wallet is a tool that allows users to interact with blockchain networks. It helps users manage their digital assets and authorize transactions.

A wallet does not usually store cryptocurrency as a file on your device. The assets are recorded on the blockchain, while the wallet manages the cryptographic keys used to access and control them.

## 2. What Is a Public Key?

A public key is a cryptographic value associated with a private key. It is used in cryptographic operations and can be used to derive a wallet address, depending on the blockchain.

Public keys and wallet addresses are not always the same thing.

A wallet address is generally shared when someone needs to send you digital assets. Always confirm that the address matches the correct network and asset.

## 3. What Is a Private Key?

A private key is a secret cryptographic value that can authorize certain blockchain transactions.

Anyone who obtains control of a private key may be able to control the associated assets. Protect it carefully.

**Never share your private key, recovery phrase, or seed phrase with anyone.**

Legitimate support staff should not need your recovery phrase to help you.

## 4. What Is a Recovery Phrase?

A recovery phrase, often called a seed phrase, is a sequence of words used by many wallets to restore access to the keys associated with a wallet.

Anyone who obtains this phrase may be able to take control of the wallet and its assets.

- Keep it private.
- Store it securely offline.
- Never enter it on an unfamiliar website.
- Never send it through chat, email, or social media.
- Be cautious of fake airdrops and wallet verification requests.

## 5. How Does a Wallet Transaction Work?

A simplified transaction process:

1. A user enters the recipient's wallet address and transaction details.
2. The wallet prepares the transaction.
3. The user reviews and approves it.
4. The wallet uses the relevant private key to sign the transaction.
5. The signed transaction is submitted to the blockchain network.
6. The network processes the transaction according to its rules.

Always verify the recipient address, blockchain network, amount, and fees before approving a transaction.

## 6. Custodial vs. Non-Custodial Wallets

**Custodial wallet:** A third party manages the private keys on the user's behalf. Access and recovery depend partly on the provider's policies.

**Non-custodial wallet:** The user controls the private keys or recovery mechanism. This provides greater direct control but also greater responsibility for protecting access.

Neither type removes every risk.

## 7. Common Wallet Security Risks

- Phishing websites and fake applications.
- Malicious smart-contract approvals.
- Fake support agents requesting recovery phrases.
- Sending assets on the wrong network.
- Malware and compromised devices.
- Approving transactions without reviewing their details.
